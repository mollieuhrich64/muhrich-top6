The Six Best New York Times Games on their Website

I have won the Wordle in one guess, and I have played the New York Times Games for years. Here are my six favorites, ranked worst to best.

Start at number six

The countdown

Six of them, worst to best

All of these games are on the New York Times site, and made my top 6 overall.

  1. 06

    Number six: The Tiles

    A colorful matching puzzle that trades wordplay for pure pattern recognition. It rounds out the top six as a fun, low-stakes palate cleanser, even if it lacks the punchy challenge of the games above it.

  2. 05

    Number five: The Midi

    The awkward middle child you play when the Mini feels too quick, but you don't have time for a full-sized grid. It lands in fifth because it packs a decent challenge, even if it feels a bit like extra homework.

  3. 04

    Number four: The Strands

    A chaotic alphabet soup that forces you to hunt for hidden themes and spanagrams. Sitting in the middle, it’s addicting to solve, even if missing a word in plain sight hurts your soul.

  4. 03

    Number three: The Connections

    A brilliantly toxic trap designed to trick you into falling for obvious bait. It holds third place because making you question your own reading comprehension is truly an art form.

  5. 02

    Number two: The Wordle

    Takes 30 seconds, boosts your ego, and never hurts your feelings. It claims top spot for giving you all the satisfaction of a full crossword without any of the headache.

  6. 01

    The best one

    Number one: The Mini

    Takes 30 seconds, boosts your ego, and never hurts your feelings. It claims top spot for giving you all the satisfaction of a full crossword without any of the headache.

Method

How I ranked these

What I judged on

When ranking my top six NYT games, I judged them on how well they balance challenge and fun. My #1 pick, The Mini, takes top honors because working through the clues is just as rewarding as finishing it. On the flip side, Tiles lands at #6 because while it’s still a blast to play, it rarely puts up much of a fight.

What I ignored

I also deliberately left the classic Crossword and Spelling Bee off my top six. The full Crossword is too much of a time commitment for a busy weekday, making it better suited for a lazy Sunday afternoon. As for Spelling Bee, it honestly feels pretty tedious unless you happen to hold a degree in linguistics and an obsession with obscure anagrams.

Where I could be wrong

While some might argue that putting The Mini behind a paywall ruins its appeal for casual players, it still easily defends the top spot on this list. Peak puzzle design is worth every penny, and paying a small subscription fee is a tiny price to pay for the unmatched high of crushing a five-by-five grid in 22 seconds flat.
